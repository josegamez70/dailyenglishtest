title: The Corner Shop
STORY
Rosa stopped at the corner shop on her way home because there was no milk and no bread in the flat. The shop was narrow, and the shelves were full. Luis, the owner, looked up from the counter and smiled at her. Rosa took a wire basket and checked the list in her hand. She chose milk, eggs, tomatoes, and a loaf of bread. Near the window, oranges sat in a wooden crate, so she picked four ripe ones. A boy next to her stood on his toes and still could not reach a tin of beans. Rosa lifted the tin down and put it in his hands. At the counter Luis entered each price on the till and turned the screen so Rosa could see the total. She opened her purse and found that she was short. Her cheeks grew warm, and she reached for the oranges to put them back. Luis said she could take the food home and bring the rest of the money the next day. Rosa did not like to owe money, but the fridge at home was empty. She agreed and wrote her name and the amount in a notebook by the till. The next evening she returned with the coins and a little card. Luis smiled and pinned the card to the board above the counter. He said trust between neighbours kept a small shop alive.

STORY
Daniel wanted a book about rivers for a school report, so he went to the college library after class. The reading room was quiet, and students sat at long tables. Meera, the librarian, stood behind the front desk with a stamp in her hand. Daniel searched the shelves and found a blue book with clear maps. Meera asked for his library card. Daniel checked his jacket pocket, then his bag, and then the pocket again. The card was not there. He remembered leaving it on his desk at home that morning. Without the card he could not borrow the book, and the report was due on Friday. Meera saw that he was worried. She asked his name and looked at the computer. His record was clean, and he had no late books. She said he could take the book for one night if he brought the card the next day. She wrote his name on a small slip and tucked the slip inside the cover. That evening he read the chapter about rivers and wrote three pages of notes. He put the library card into his bag before he went to bed so he would not forget it. In the morning he returned to the desk, showed the card, and Meera stamped the book in the normal way. Daniel learned that a careful librarian can help, but a card still belongs in your bag.

title: A Group Project
STORY
Hana, Felix, and Omar had to prepare a short talk about their town for history class. They met in an empty classroom after lunch and closed the door so they could think. Hana opened a folder and spread three pages of notes on the table. Felix had photos of the old market on his phone, and Omar had a map he had drawn at home. At first they all spoke at once, and nobody wrote anything down. Hana asked them to take turns. She said the talk could last only five minutes, so each person needed one clear part. Omar offered to explain the map. Hana offered to begin and to end the talk. They wrote a simple plan in the folder and numbered the steps. Omar pointed at the map and spoke too fast, so Hana tapped the table and he slowed down. Felix forgot a date, and Omar found it in the notes. They practised the whole talk twice. The second time it fitted the five minutes and sounded calm. Before they left, Hana checked that every photo and the map were in the folder. Felix promised to charge his phone. Omar promised to bring the folder in the morning so nobody would leave it behind. The teacher smiled when the talk finished on time. The three friends learned that a plan and a little patience can turn a noisy group into a team.

title: The Missed Train
STORY
Noah arrived at the station with his ticket in his hand and his bag on his shoulder. He needed the train that left at ten. A crowd stood in the hall, and the board above the doors showed many times. Noah found his platform and walked quickly down the stairs. When he reached the platform, the doors of the train were already closing. The train left without him. His ticket was for that train, and the next one was an hour later. He went back upstairs to the ticket desk. Yusuf, who worked there, listened to the story and looked at the ticket. He said the missed train could not be stopped, but Noah could use the same ticket on the next train that day. Noah thanked him and asked where he could wait. Yusuf pointed to the station cafe and said a cup of tea might make the hour feel shorter. Noah bought tea and sat by the window. He took out a book he had packed for the journey and read a whole chapter. The wait was boring at first, then it became quiet and useful. When the next train was announced, Noah checked the platform on the board and walked down again. He chose a seat, put his bag on the rack, and kept his ticket where he could see it. As the train left, he thought about arriving at stations with time to spare.
