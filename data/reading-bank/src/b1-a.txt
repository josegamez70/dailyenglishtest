title: The Lost House Key
STORY
Marta left her flat at eight and walked to the small market on her street. She locked the front door and dropped the house key into her coat pocket. The morning was cool, and the bread stall already had a short queue. Marta bought a loaf and a bag of apples. She paid, said thank you, and turned toward home. At the corner she put her hand into the pocket. The key was not there. She stopped and checked the other pocket, then her bag, and then the ground by her feet. She felt hot and worried. Her neighbour Pedro was coming out of the shop. He saw her face and asked what was wrong. Marta said she had lost the house key. Pedro walked back to the bread stall with her. The seller looked under the counter and held up one key. It had been on the ground beside the apples. Later Marta tried it in her own door, and the lock turned. She thanked Pedro and the seller. That evening she fixed a small hook on the wall by the door. She hung the key there before she went to bed. The next morning the key was still on the hook, and Marta left home calm and on time.

title: A Noisy Morning
STORY
Leo woke up before his alarm because someone was drilling in the flat above. The noise started, stopped, and started again. He put a pillow over his head, but he could still hear it. At seven he got up and made tea. The drilling did not stop. Leo did not want to shout. He wrote a short note that said the noise began very early. He went upstairs and knocked on the door. A woman called Nora opened it with dust on her hands. She looked sorry at once. She said she was putting up a shelf before work and did not know the sound was so loud. Leo showed her the note and pointed at the time. Nora turned off the drill. She promised to wait until nine the next day. Leo thanked her and went back downstairs. His flat was quiet again. He drank his tea and packed his bag. Before he left, Nora slipped a second note under his door. It said she would finish the shelf at the weekend, after nine. Leo smiled. A calm talk had worked better than anger. He opened the window and heard only a bird in the yard. The shelf could wait until a kinder hour.

pillow | a soft cushion for your head
upstairs | on a higher floor
sorry | feeling bad about what you did
promised | said firmly that you will do something
anger | a strong feeling of being cross

title: The New Neighbour
STORY
Aisha saw boxes in the hall on Saturday morning. A new neighbour was moving into the empty flat. His name was Sam. He carried a lamp, a suitcase, and a plant with long leaves. The plant nearly fell when he opened his door. Aisha held the door and took the plant. Sam thanked her and said he did not know anyone in the building yet. Aisha told him her name and the number of her flat. She also told him that the bins were behind the back door. Sam wrote it in a small notebook. Later she brought him a cup of tea and two biscuits. They stood in the hall because his chairs were still in the van. Sam said he worked at the hospital and started early. Aisha said the street was quiet at night. Before she left, she pointed to the shelf where people left parcels. Sam put his spare key in a drawer, not under the mat. He said he would say hello when he saw her on the stairs. Aisha went home glad that the empty flat was no longer empty. She washed the two cups and left them on the rack. It felt good to know the name of the person next door.

neighbour | a person who lives near you
suitcase | a bag for carrying clothes on a trip
parcels | packages that people send
spare | extra, kept for later
drawer | a box that slides out of a table
glad | pleased and happy

title: Washing Day
STORY
On Sunday Elena carried a basket of wet clothes down to the courtyard. The sun was out, and the washing line was free. She shook each shirt and hung it with a wooden peg. A blue shirt belonged to her brother Hugo. A white towel belonged to her. The wind was light, so the clothes moved but did not fall. At noon a cloud covered the sun. Elena looked up and decided to wait. Ten minutes later the cloud passed. She turned the shirts so the other side could dry. Her neighbour Ines came out with her own basket and asked if there was room. Elena moved the towel along the line and made a space. Ines thanked her and hung two pillowcases. They talked about the weather while they worked. By four o'clock the clothes were dry. Elena folded them into the basket and took the pegs off the line. She left the line clear for the next person. Upstairs, Hugo looked for his blue shirt and found it on the chair, warm and smooth. Elena put the pegs in a tin so they would not get lost. The courtyard was empty again, and the line moved gently in the wind.

VOCAB
courtyard | an open space inside a building
peg | a small clip that holds washing
towel | a cloth for drying your body
folded | laid one part over another 
pillowcases | covers for pillows
smooth | flat and soft, not rough
